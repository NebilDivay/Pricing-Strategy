# Tiered Pricing Strategy & Profitability Analysis

## Project Overview

This project focuses on developing a **tiered pricing strategy for creative marketing services at HOBO Creative Marketing**.

The objective was to structure service packages based on **production hours, resource allocation, management fees, and company profit targets**, while making higher-tier packages more attractive to customers.

The pricing model introduced three service packages:

- **Silver**
- **Gold**
- **Platinum**

Each tier offers increasing quantities of **video editing and poster design services** at progressively higher price points.

The overall strategy was designed to balance **customer value with business profitability**, while creating clear opportunities for **upselling customers to higher-value packages**.

---

# Business Objectives

The main objectives of the project were to:

- Develop structured pricing packages based on production effort and service requirements.
- Establish consistent pricing for individual creative deliverables.
- Allocate package revenue across production, social media management, and advertising management.
- Maintain a target company profit margin across different packages.
- Create packages suitable for customers with different budgets and content requirements.
- Encourage customers to upgrade by offering better value at higher package tiers.
- Increase potential revenue per customer through a structured upselling strategy.

---

# 1. Production Cost Analysis

The first step was to estimate the **production time required for each creative deliverable** and establish a corresponding cost allocation.

| Service | Production Time | Allocation |
|---|---:|---:|
| Poster Design | 1 hour | ₹350 |
| Video Editing | 2 hours | ₹700 |

The allocation was based on an estimated production rate of:

**₹350 per production hour**

### Poster Design

```text
1 hour × ₹350 = ₹350
```

### Video Editing

```text
2 hours × ₹350 = ₹700
```

This established a consistent, time-based methodology for calculating the creative production component of each service package.

---

# 2. Additional Service Allocations

In addition to creative production, each package included two recurring service allocations.

| Service | Allocation |
|---|---:|
| Social Media Management | ₹1,200 |
| Ad Management | ₹900 |
| **Total** | **₹2,100** |

These allocations remained consistent across all three packages.

> **Note:** The ₹900 allocation covers **advertising management labour only**. Actual advertising expenditure or media spend is paid separately by the customer.

# 3. Package Development & Pricing

Three packages were developed based on different levels of creative output.

## Silver Package - ₹7,500

The Silver package provides an entry-level option for customers requiring a smaller volume of monthly creative content.

| Component | Quantity / Allocation | Amount |
|---|---:|---:|
| Poster Design | 4 × ₹350 | ₹1,400 |
| Video Editing | 4 × ₹700 | ₹2,800 |
| Social Media Management | Fixed | ₹1,200 |
| Ad Management | Fixed | ₹900 |
| Company Profit Allocation | - | ₹1,200 |
| **Total Package Price** | | **₹7,500** |

### Calculation

```text
₹1,400
+ ₹2,800
+ ₹1,200
+ ₹900
+ ₹1,200
= ₹7,500
```

---

| Component | Quantity / Allocation | Amount |
|---|---:|---:|

| Social Media Management | Fixed | ₹1,200 |
| Ad Management | Fixed | ₹900 |
| Company Profit Allocation | - | ₹2,000 |
| **Total Package Price** | | **₹12,500** |

### Calculation

```text
₹2,800
+ ₹5,600
+ ₹1,200
+ ₹900
+ ₹2,000
= ₹12,500
```

---

## Platinum Package - ₹17,500

The Platinum package provides the highest creative output and is designed for customers requiring a larger volume of content.

| Component | Quantity / Allocation | Amount |
|---|---:|---:|

```text
₹4,200
+ ₹8,400

```

# 4. Package Comparison

The final package structure was:

| Package | Posters | Videos | Total Creative Deliverables | Package Price |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|

The package structure provides customers with progressively higher quantities of creative content while maintaining a simple three-tier offering.

---

# 5. Pricing Strategy: Encouraging Upselling

A key consideration when developing the pricing strategy was ensuring that customers received **better value when upgrading to a higher package**.

Rather than increasing the package price proportionally with the number of creative deliverables, higher packages provide significantly more content for a less-than-proportional increase in price.

---

## Silver to Gold Upgrade

### Deliverable Increase

Silver provides:

**8 creative deliverables**

Gold provides:

**16 creative deliverables**

Percentage increase:

```text
((16 - 8) / 8) × 100
= 100%
```

The customer receives **100% more creative deliverables**.

### Price Increase

```text
((₹12,500 - ₹7,500) / ₹7,500) × 100
= 66.7%
```

The package price increases by only **66.7%**.

### Business Insight

Customers receive **twice the creative output** while the package price increases by less than twice the Silver package price.

This creates a clear incentive for customers to consider upgrading from Silver to Gold.

---

## Silver to Platinum Upgrade

### Deliverable Increase

Silver provides:

**8 creative deliverables**

Platinum provides:

**24 creative deliverables**

Percentage increase:

```text
((24 - 8) / 8) × 100
= 200%
```

The customer receives **200% more creative deliverables**.

### Price Increase

```text
((₹17,500 - ₹7,500) / ₹7,500) × 100
= 133.3%
```

The package price increases by **133.3%**.

### Business Insight

The Platinum package provides **three times the number of creative deliverables** compared with Silver, while the customer does not pay three times the Silver package price.

This strengthens the value proposition of the higher-tier package.

Another way to evaluate the pricing strategy is by comparing the package price with the number of creative deliverables included.

Package Price / Total Creative Deliverables

The declining package price per creative deliverable demonstrates how customers receive increasing value when moving to a higher package tier.

# 7. Profitability Analysis

The pricing model allocated a consistent **16% of package revenue** to planned company profit.

| Package | Package Price | Profit Allocation | Profit Margin |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Silver | ₹7,500 | ₹1,200 | 16% |
| Gold | ₹12,500 | ₹2,000 | 16% |
| Platinum | ₹17,500 | ₹2,800 | 16% |

### Profit Margin Formula

```text
Profit Margin = (Profit Allocation / Package Price) × 100
```

### Silver

```text
(₹1,200 / ₹7,500) × 100
= 16%
```

### Gold

```text
(₹2,000 / ₹12,500) × 100
= 16%
```

### Platinum

```text
(₹2,800 / ₹17,500) × 100
= 16%
```

Maintaining the same target profit margin across all three packages provides a consistent profitability objective while allowing the **absolute profit allocation to increase with package value**.

> **Important:** These figures represent planned profit allocations within the pricing model. Actual net profitability depends on all operating expenses, overhead, software subscriptions, employee costs, taxes, and other delivery expenses being fully accounted for.

---

# 8. Profit Growth Across Package Tiers

Although the planned profit margin remains constant at **16%**, the absolute profit allocation increases as customers move to higher packages.

| Package | Planned Profit Allocation |
|---|---:|
| Silver | ₹1,200 |
| Gold | ₹2,000 |
| Platinum | ₹2,800 |

### Silver to Gold

```text
₹2,000 - ₹1,200 = ₹800
```

Moving a customer from Silver to Gold increases planned profit allocation by:

**₹800 per customer**

### Silver to Platinum

```text
₹2,800 - ₹1,200 = ₹1,600
```

Moving a customer from Silver to Platinum increases planned profit allocation by:

**₹1,600 per customer**

This demonstrates the commercial benefit of successfully upselling customers while maintaining the same target profit margin.

# 9. Revenue Growth Opportunity

The tiered structure provides an opportunity to increase **average revenue per customer**.

For example:

- Silver customer revenue = **₹7,500**
- Gold customer revenue = **₹12,500**
- Platinum customer revenue = **₹17,500**

Moving a customer from Silver to Gold increases revenue by:

```text
₹12,500 - ₹7,500
= ₹5,000
```

Moving a customer from Silver to Platinum increases revenue by:

```text
₹17,500 - ₹7,500
= ₹10,000
```

Because social media management and ad management allocations remain fixed across the packages, the pricing structure creates an opportunity to increase revenue through additional creative production while avoiding a proportional increase in these management allocations.

# 10. Key Business Insights

The pricing model demonstrated several important commercial principles.

### 1. Time-Based Cost Allocation

Production hours provide a consistent basis for estimating the creative production component of each package.

Using a common rate of **₹350 per production hour** creates a structured approach to allocating production value.

### 2. Tiered Pricing

Silver, Gold, and Platinum packages cater to customers with different:

- Content requirements
- Marketing needs
- Creative volumes
- Budgets

### 3. Value-Based Upselling

Higher packages provide more deliverables for a less-than-proportional increase in price.

This creates a stronger incentive for customers to upgrade.

### 4. Profit Margin Management

A planned company profit allocation of **16%** is incorporated into each package.

This creates a consistent profitability target across the pricing structure.

### 5. Revenue Growth Opportunity

Encouraging customers to choose Gold or Platinum can increase revenue per customer while fixed management allocations remain unchanged.

### 6. Clearer Customer Choice

Using three clearly defined packages simplifies the purchasing decision by giving customers structured options rather than requiring every service engagement to be priced independently.

# Conclusion

This project demonstrates how **production effort, service allocation, pricing strategy, and profitability objectives** can be combined to develop a structured service-pricing model.

By introducing **Silver, Gold, and Platinum packages**, the business created a clearer service offering, established consistent pricing allocations, and provided customers with an incentive to select higher-value packages.

The model balances two important commercial objectives:

1. **Providing customers with stronger value at higher package tiers**
2. **Maintaining a consistent target profit allocation for the business**

The analysis also demonstrates how tiered pricing can be used as an **upselling strategy**, allowing customers to receive significantly more creative output without experiencing a proportional increase in package price.

## Central Takeaway

> **Effective pricing is not simply about calculating the cost of delivering a service. It is about structuring an offer that creates value for customers while supporting sustainable business profitability.**
